## Beyond a Single Population

*   Most species do not exist as one continuous population.
*   Their habitat is fragmented into patches (e.g., lakes for fish, forests for birds, mountain tops for plants).
*   A **metapopulation** is "a population of populations" connected by dispersal.

---

## Definition: Metapopulation

*   A set of local populations occupying discrete habitat patches, linked by the **immigration and emigration** of individuals.
*   Key idea: Local populations can go **extinct**, but the patch can be **re-colonized** from another patch.

---

## The Levins Metapopulation Model

*   A simple, classic model that tracks the **proportion of occupied patches (p)** over time.
*   $ \frac{dp}{dt} = iP(1-P) - eP $
    *   $i$: Colonization rate
    *   $e$: Extinction rate
    *   $P$: Fraction of patches occupied
*   At equilibrium: $ \hat{P} = 1 - \frac{e}{i} $

---

## Core Concepts of the Model

*   **Metapopulation persistence requires that colonization rate > extinction rate ($i > e$).**
*   Even if every local population has a high risk of extinction, the metapopulation as a whole can persist.
*   It's a balance between local extinctions and re-colonizations.

---

## Real-World Example: The Bay Checkerspot Butterfly

*   Studied by ecologist Paul Ehrlich and others.
*   Lives on serpentine soil patches in California grasslands.
*   Local populations on patches frequently go extinct due to drought, disease, or food shortage.
*   The species persists because empty patches are re-colonized by butterflies from larger, more stable ("source") populations.

---

## Source vs. Sink Populations

*   **Source Population:** A high-quality habitat where local reproductive success is greater than mortality ($R_0 > 1$). This population **produces emigrants**.
*   **Sink Population:** A low-quality habitat where local reproductive success is less than mortality ($R_0 < 1$). This population **requires immigrants** to persist.
*   A metapopulation can be a mix of source and sink patches.

---

## Importance of Metapopulations

*   **Conservation Biology:** Explains how species can persist in fragmented landscapes (e.g., due to human development). Highlights the need for **habitat corridors** to facilitate dispersal.
*   **Rescue Effect:** Immigration from a source can prevent a small sink population from going extinct.
*   **Designing Reserves:** A network of several small reserves may be better than one large one if they are connected by dispersal.

---

## Summary

*   The metapopulation concept scales up from local populations to the regional level.
*   **Dispersal and connectivity** are as important as birth and death rates for species survival.
*   Local extinction is a normal process, not always a tragedy, if re-colonization is possible.
