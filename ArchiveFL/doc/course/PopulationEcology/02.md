## Density-Independent Growth

*   Population growth that is **not** influenced by the size of the population itself.
*   The per capita growth rate remains constant.
*   Driven primarily by **abiotic factors** (e.g., weather, disasters).
*   Also called "exponential growth."

---

## The Exponential Growth Equation

$$ N_t = N_0 e^{rt} $$

*   $ N_t $ = Population size at time *t*
*   $ N_0 $ = Initial population size
*   $ e $ = Base of the natural logarithm (~2.718)
*   $ r $ = **Intrinsic rate of increase** (per capita birth rate - per capita death rate)
*   $ t $ = Time

---

## Understanding 'r': The Intrinsic Rate of Increase

*   $ r = b - d $
    *   $ b $ = per capita birth rate
    *   $ d $ = per capita death rate
*   **If r > 0:** Population is growing.
*   **If r = 0:** Population is stable (ZPG).
*   **If r < 0:** Population is declining.

---

## The Power of Exponential Growth: An Example

*   Start with 10 bacteria ($N_0 = 10$).
*   They divide every 20 minutes ($r$ is very high!).
*   How many after 6 hours (18 generations)?

$$ N_t = 10 \times 2^{18} $$
$$ N_t = 10 \times 262,144 $$
$$ N_t = 2,621,440 $$

**In just 6 hours, one cell becomes over 2.6 million!**

---

## Real-World Examples of Exponential Growth

*   **Invasive Species:** Initially, when a species is introduced to a new habitat with no predators/parasites (e.g., rabbits in Australia, zebra mussels in the Great Lakes).
*   **Bacteria in a New Flask:** Growth in a lab under ideal conditions.
*   **Human Population:** For much of the last few centuries, the global human population grew at an exponential rate.

---

## Assumptions of the Exponential Model

*   **No resource limitation:** Food, space, nutrients are unlimited.
*   **No negative interactions:** No disease, no predation, no competition.
*   **Constant b and d:** Birth and death rates do not change.
*   **No population structure:** All individuals are identical (no age or sex differences).
*   **Continuous growth & reproduction:** Not realistic for all species.

---

## When is this Model Useful?

*   **Short-term predictions** for populations well below carrying capacity.
*   Modeling populations in **ideal, controlled environments** (lab settings).
*   **Theoretical baseline** to compare against more complex models.
*   **Conservation:** Estimating the potential growth of a recovering population.

---

## Limitations of the Model

*   **No population can grow exponentially forever.**
*   Resources always become limited.
*   Density-independent factors (like a storm) might wipe out the population unpredictably.
*   It ignores biology! (competition, predation, etc.)

---

## Looking Ahead

*   Exponential growth is a useful starting point, but it's not realistic.
*   In nature, growth is limited by resources and the population itself.
