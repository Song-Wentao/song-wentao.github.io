## What is a Life History?

*   The schedule of an organism's life: key events related to growth, reproduction, and survival.
*   **Key Traits:**
    *   **Age at first reproduction**
    *   **Number of reproductive events** (semelparity vs. iteroparity)
    *   **Number of offspring per event** (fecundity)
    *   **Parental investment** per offspring
    *   **Lifespan**

---

## The Fundamental Trade-off

*   Organisms have limited resources (energy, time).
*   They cannot maximize all traits simultaneously.
*   **Trade-off:** Energy allocated to one function (e.g., growth) cannot be allocated to another (e.g., reproduction or repair).

---

## r-selected vs. K-selected Strategies

*   A classic framework for understanding life history variation.

| Trait | r-Selected Species | K-Selected Species |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Habitat** | Unstable, unpredictable | Stable, predictable |
| **Mortality** | Often density-independent, high | Often density-dependent, low |
| **Lifespan** | Short | Long |
| **Reproduction** | Early, many small offspring | Late, few large offspring |
| **Parental Care** | None | Extensive |
| **Examples** | Insects, weeds, bacteria | Elephants, whales, humans, trees |

---

## Semelparity vs. Iteroparity

*   **Semelparity:** "Big Bang" reproduction. Reproduce once and die.
    *   *Example:* Pacific Salmon, agave plants, annual plants.
    *   Advantage: Put all energy into one massive reproductive effort.
*   **Iteroparity:** Repeated reproduction over multiple seasons.
    *   *Example:* Most vertebrates, perennial plants.
    *   Advantage: "Bet-hedging" - multiple chances to reproduce over a lifetime.

---

## The Principle of Allocation

*   **Trade-off: Number vs. Quality of Offspring**
    *   Produce many small offspring with low survival chance (r-strategy).
    *   OR produce a few large offspring with high survival chance (K-strategy).
*   **Trade-off: Current vs. Future Reproduction**
    *   Reproduce now → might reduce growth/survival → lower future reproduction.
    *   Delay reproduction → grow larger → may have higher fecundity later.

---

## Case Study: Dandelion vs. Oak Tree

*   **Dandelion (r-selected):**
    *   Produces 1000s of tiny seeds dispersed by wind.
    *   No parental care.
    *   Grows quickly in disturbed soil.
    *   Short lifespan.
*   **Oak Tree (K-selected):**
    *   Produces 100s of large seeds (acorns) with stored energy.
    *   Heavy investment in each offspring.
    *   Grows slowly.
    *   Very long lifespan.

---

## The Grime's Triangle (for Plants)

*   Another model focusing on plant strategies in response to stress and disturbance:
    *   **Ruderal (R):** Weeds. High disturbance, low stress. (Similar to r-selected).
    *   **Stress-Tolerators (S):** Live in harsh conditions (e.g., shade, low nutrients). Slow growth, evergreen.
    *   **Competitors (C):** Dominate in productive, low-disturbance areas. Fast growth, tall.

---

## Summary

*   Life histories are evolved solutions to ecological challenges.
*   They represent **trade-offs** shaped by natural selection.
*   There is no "best" strategy—the best strategy depends on the environment.
