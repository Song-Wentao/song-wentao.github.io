# Lecture 02: The Raw Material: Mutation & Genetic Variation
**Textbook Chapter:** Genetic Variation
**Key Concepts:** Mutation, Genetic Variation, Neutral Theory, Types of Mutations

---

## 1. Where Does Variation Come From?
*   **Ultimate Source:** **Mutation** - a change in the DNA sequence.
*   **Recombination** (during meiosis) shuffles existing mutations into new combinations.
*   Without variation, natural selection has nothing to act upon.

---

## 2. Types of Mutations
*   **Point Mutation:** A change in a single nucleotide (e.g., A -> T).
    *   **Synonymous ("silent"):** Codes for the same amino acid. Often neutral.
    *   **Non-synonymous ("missense"):** Codes for a different amino acid. Can be neutral, harmful, or beneficial.
    *   **Nonsense:** Creates a premature stop codon. Usually harmful.
*   **Insertions/Deletions (Indels):** Adding or removing nucleotides. Often causes frameshift, which is usually harmful.
*   **Gene Duplication:** Entire gene is copied. Major source of new genetic material (e.g., globin gene family).
*   **Chromosomal Mutations:** Deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation of large chromosome chunks.

---

## 3. The Neutral Theory of Molecular Evolution (Motoo Kimura)
*   **Core Idea:** The vast majority of mutations that become fixed in a population are **neutral**—they have no effect on fitness.
*   They spread through populations by random chance (**genetic drift**), not natural selection.
*   **Why important?** Provides a **"molecular clock"** - the rate of neutral mutation accumulation is roughly constant over time, allowing us to date evolutionary events.

---

## 4. Measuring Genetic Variation
*   Not all variation is visible (like eye color).
*   How do we measure it?
    *   **Protein Electrophoresis:** Measures variation in protein sequences (older technique).
    *   **DNA Sequencing:** Directly reads the DNA code. The gold standard. Allows us to find **Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs)**.
    *   **Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS):** Link genetic variants to traits/diseases.

---

## 5. Examples of Genetic Variation in Action
*   **Sickle Cell Anemia:** A single point mutation (missense) in the hemoglobin gene.
    *   **Heterozygote Advantage:** Having one copy (AS) provides resistance to malaria, while two copies (SS) causes the disease. A classic example of balancing selection maintaining variation.
*   **Lactose Persistence:** Mutation in the regulatory region of the lactase gene allows adults to digest milk. Strong positive selection in cultures with a history of dairy farming.
*   **CCR5-Δ32 Mutation:** A 32-base pair deletion in the CCR5 gene provides resistance to HIV infection.
