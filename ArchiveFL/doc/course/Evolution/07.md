# Lecture 07: Reading the History of Life: Phylogenetics
**Textbook Chapter:** Phylogenetics
**Key Concepts:** Phylogeny, Cladistics, Synapomorphy, Monophyletic Groups, Molecular Clocks

---

## 1. The Family Tree of Life
*   **Phylogeny:** The evolutionary history of a species or group of species.
*   **Phylogenetic Tree:** A hypothesis about the evolutionary relationships among organisms.
*   **Components of a Tree:**
    *   **Tips:** Represent living species or taxa.
    *   **Nodes:** Represent common ancestors.
    *   **Branches:** Represent lineages through time.
    *   **Clade:** A group that includes a common ancestor and all of its descendants.

---

## 2. How Do We Build Trees? The Principle of Cladistics
*   **Goal:** Group organisms by their **shared evolutionary history**.
*   We use **shared derived characteristics** (**Synapomorphies**) to identify clades.
    *   **Ancestral Trait:** Present in the common ancestor of a group (e.g., backbone in mammals).
    *   **Derived Trait:** Evolved in a specific lineage after it split from the common ancestor (e.g., hair in mammals).
*   **Example:** Having hair is a synapomorphy that defines the mammal clade.

---

## 3. Key Groupings
*   **Monophyletic Group (Clade):** An ancestor and ALL of its descendants. This is the only "natural" grouping we accept. **(GOOD)**
    *   *Example:* Mammalia.
*   **Paraphyletic Group:** An ancestor and SOME of its descendants. **(BAD)**
    *   *Example:* "Reptilia" if it doesn't include birds (which are descended from dinosaurs, a type of reptile).
*   **Polyphyletic Group:** A group that does not include the common ancestor of all members. **(VERY BAD)**
    *   *Example:* "Flying vertebrates" (birds, bats, pterosaurs) evolved flight independently.

---

## 4. How to Read a Phylogenetic Tree
*   **Important:** Tips can be rotated around any node without changing the meaning.
*   **Time** is represented along the branches, not across the tips. The length of a branch can represent time or amount of change.
*   **Closely related** means sharing a **recent common ancestor**. It does NOT mean "looks similar."
    *   *Example:* Crocodiles are more closely related to birds than they are to lizards, even though they look more like lizards.

---

## 5. Molecular Phylogenetics
*   We can use DNA or protein sequences as characters for building trees.
*   **More data!** Genomes provide millions of characters (base pairs) to compare.
*   Allows us to build trees for organisms with few morphological traits (e.g., bacteria) or to test morphological hypotheses.

---

## 6. The Molecular Clock
*   **Assumption:** Some regions of the genome evolve at a roughly constant rate (neutral theory).
*   **Calibration:** Using the fossil record, we can date a split (e.g., mammals and reptiles ~310 mya). This sets the "tick rate" of the clock.
*   **Application:** We can use the amount of genetic difference between two living species to **estimate the time of their divergence**.
    *   *Example:* Estimating when humans and chimpanzees last shared a common ancestor (~6-7 mya).
