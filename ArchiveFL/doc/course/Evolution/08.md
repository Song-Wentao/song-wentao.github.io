# Lecture 08: The Molecular Footprints of Evolution
**Textbook Chapter:** Molecular Evolution
**Key Concepts:** Genome Evolution, Gene Families, Evo-Devo, Hox Genes, Gene Regulation

---

## 1. Evolution at the Molecular Level
*   The genome itself is a historical document, filled with evidence of past evolutionary events.

---

## 2. How Genomes Evolve
*   **Gene Duplication:** The major source of new genetic material.
    *   **Ohno's Model:** Duplicated genes are initially redundant. One copy can accumulate mutations without harming the organism.
    *   **Possible Fates:**
        1.  **Non-functionalization:** Becomes a pseudogene (broken copy).
        2.  **Neofunctionalization:** Evolves a completely new function.
        3.  **Subfunctionalization:** The original function is divided between the two copies.
*   **Example - Globin Gene Family:** A single ancestral gene duplicated and diverged into myoglobin, alpha-globin, beta-globin, etc., each with specialized functions (oxygen storage vs. transport).

---

## 3. Evolutionary Developmental Biology ("Evo-Devo")
*   The study of how changes in **development** lead to evolutionary changes in **form**.
*   **Key Insight:** Major morphological changes can be caused by small changes in genes that control the *timing*, *location*, or *amount* of development (**regulatory genes**).

---

## 4. The Genetic Toolkit: Hox Genes
*   A set of **highly conserved** master regulatory genes that determine the anterior-posterior (head-to-tail) body axis in animals.
*   They are **transcription factors** that turn on cascades of other genes.
*   **How they produce diversity:** Changes in the *regulation* of Hox genes (when and where they are expressed) can create drastic changes in body plans.
    *   *Example:* In snakes, the expression domain of certain Hox genes is extended, leading to the development of many more vertebrae and ribs.
    *   *Example:* The evolution of limbs in tetrapods is linked to the expression of specific Hox genes.

---

## 5. Deep Homology
*   The discovery that distantly related organisms share the same ancient genetic machinery for building body parts.
*   *Example:* The *Pax6* gene is a master control gene for eye development. The same gene can induce eye formation in flies, mice, and squid! This suggests all animal eyes share a common genetic ancestor, even though the anatomical structures evolved independently.

---

## 6. Junk DNA is a Treasure Trove
*   **Pseudogenes:** "Dead" genes that have been inactivated by mutation. They are powerful evidence for common ancestry (e.g., GULO pseudogene in primates that can't make Vitamin C).
*   **Endogenous Retroviruses (ERVs):** Viral DNA that inserted into the germline of an ancestor and was inherited. Shared ERV insertions in the same genomic location are strong evidence of common descent (e.g., humans and chimps share specific ERVs).
