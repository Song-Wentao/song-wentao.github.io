# Lecture 03: The Mechanisms of Evolutionary Change
**Textbook Chapter:** Forces of Evolution
**Key Concepts:** Natural Selection, Genetic Drift, Gene Flow, Mutation, Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

---

## 1. The Four Forces of Evolution
Evolution is a change in allele frequencies in a population over time. Four mechanisms cause this change:
1.  **Natural Selection**
2.  **Genetic Drift**
3.  **Gene Flow**
4.  **Mutation**

---

## 2. Natural Selection Revisited
*   **Differential survival and reproduction** of individuals due to differences in phenotype.
*   The only mechanism that consistently produces **adaptations**.
*   **Three Modes:**
    *   **Directional Selection:** Favors one extreme phenotype (e.g., larger size).
    *   **Stabilizing Selection:** Favors the average phenotype, reduces variation (e.g., human birth weight).
    *   **Disruptive Selection:** Favors both extremes, selects against the average (can lead to speciation).

---

## 3. Genetic Drift
*   **Random change** in allele frequencies due to **sampling error** in finite populations.
*   **Strongest in small populations.**
*   **Not adaptive!** It can cause harmful alleles to increase or beneficial ones to be lost.
*   **Founder Effect:** A new population is started by a small number of individuals, carrying only a fraction of the original population's genetic variation.
    *   *Example:* Amish communities and Ellis-van Creveld syndrome.
*   **Population Bottleneck:** A sharp reduction in population size (e.g., by disaster) reduces genetic variation.
    *   *Example:* Northern Elephant Seals hunted to ~20 individuals; now recovered but with almost no genetic variation.

---

## 4. Gene Flow (Migration)
*   The transfer of genetic variation **between populations**.
*   Tends to **reduce genetic differences** between populations, homogenizing them.
*   Can introduce new alleles into a population.
*   *Example:* Pollen being blown by wind to a new meadow; animals moving between herds.

---

## 5. Mutation
*   The ultimate source of **new alleles**.
*   Its role as an evolutionary force is very weak on its own because mutation rates are low.
*   Its power is in **creating the variation** that the other forces (selection, drift) can act upon.

---

## 6. The Null Model: Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium
*   A mathematical model that describes a population that is **NOT evolving**.
*   **Conditions (5 Assumptions):**
    1.  No Natural Selection
    2.  No Genetic Drift (infinitely large population)
    3.  No Gene Flow
    4.  No Mutation
    5.  Random Mating
*   **Equation:** p² + 2pq + q² = 1
    *   p = frequency of allele A
    *   q = frequency of allele a
    *   p² = frequency of genotype AA
    *   2pq = frequency of genotype Aa
    *   q² = frequency of genotype aa
*   **Why it's useful:** It's a null hypothesis. If observed genotype frequencies differ from H-W expectations, we know one or more evolutionary forces are at work.
