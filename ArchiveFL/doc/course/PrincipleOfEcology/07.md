### 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, you will be able to:
*   Distinguish between herbivory and predation, and describe plant defenses.
*   Define parasitism, and differentiate it from parasitoidism.
*   Explain the concept of mutualism and provide key examples.
*   Describe how species interactions can evolve over time.

---

### 2. Herbivory (+/-)

*   **Definition:** A consumer (herbivore) eats part of a plant or alga.
*   **It's not always fatal:** Unlike typical predation, the prey (plant) often survives.
*   **Plant Defenses:**
    *   **Physical:** Thorns, spines, prickles, tough leaves (e.g., cellulose, lignin).
    *   **Chemical:** Toxins (e.g., caffeine, nicotine, cyanide), tannins that reduce digestibility.
*   **Coevolutionary Arms Race:** Plants evolve better defenses → herbivores evolve counter-adaptations (e.g., specialized detoxification systems).

---

### 3. Parasitism (+/-)

*   **Definition:** One organism (the parasite) derives its nourishment from another (the host), which is harmed in the process.
*   **The parasite lives in or on the host.**
*   *Examples:* Ticks, tapeworms, mistletoe, pathogenic bacteria.
*   **Parasitoidism:** A special type where an insect (e.g., a wasp) lays eggs in a host, and the larvae eventually consume and kill the host. It's like a slow, internal predation.

---

### 4. Mutualism (+/+)

*   **Definition:** An interspecific interaction that benefits both species.
*   **Obligate Mutualism:** One species cannot survive without the other.
*   **Facultative Mutualism:** Both species can survive alone, but benefit from the interaction.

#### Iconic Examples

*   **Pollination:** Animal gets food (nectar/pollen); plant gets pollen transferred.
*   **Lichen:** Fungus (provides structure, moisture) + Alga or Cyanobacterium (provides food via photosynthesis).
*   **Mycorrhizae:** Fungus (gets sugars) + Plant roots (gets increased surface area for water/nutrient absorption).
*   **Gut Microbiome:** You (digestion, immunity) + Bacteria (get a place to live and food).

---

### 5. Commensalism (+/0) & Amensalism (-/0)

*   **Commensalism:** One species benefits, the other is unaffected.
    *   *Example: Birds nesting in a tree. The bird benefits (shelter), the tree is unaffected.*
    *   *Challenging to prove true neutrality.*
*   **Amensalism:** One species is harmed, the other is unaffected.
    *   *Example: A large elephant walking on and crushing grass. The grass is harmed, the elephant is unaffected.*

---

### 6. The Blurred Lines of Interactions

*   Interactions are not always fixed; they can change depending on context.
*   **Example: Cleaner Fish**
    *   Normally mutualistic: Cleaner fish gets food; client fish gets parasites removed.
    *   But if the cleaner fish cheats and eats healthy tissue, the interaction becomes parasitic (+/-).
*   This context-dependence is a major area of ecological research.
