### 2. Carrying Capacity (K)

*   **Definition:** The maximum population size that a particular environment can sustain indefinitely.
*   **Why it exists:** Resources are finite (food, space, nutrients, light). Waste products accumulate. Predators, parasites, and diseases become more effective.
*   **Symbol:** **K**

---

### 3. Logistic Growth: Reality Sets In

*   **Assumption:** Resources are limited. Growth is constrained by the carrying capacity.
*   **Pattern:** S-shaped (sigmoidal) curve. Growth slows as the population approaches K.
*   **The Equation:**
    *   `dN / dt = r * N * [(K - N) / K]`
    *   The new term `(K - N)/K` represents the fraction of the carrying capacity that is still "available".
    *   When N is small, growth is nearly exponential.
    *   When N is close to K, growth slows to nearly zero.

---

### 4. What Stops Growth? Limiting Factors

*   **Density-Dependent Factors:** Their effect *depends* on population density. They intensify as the population grows.
    *   *Examples:* Competition for resources, predation, disease, parasitism, waste accumulation.
    *   **They are biotic factors and provide regulation.**
*   **Density-Independent Factors:** Their effect is *independent* of population density. They affect a similar percentage of individuals regardless of size.
    *   *Examples:* Weather events (fires, hurricanes, droughts), natural disasters, pollution.
    *   **They are often abiotic factors and can cause drastic crashes.**

---

### 5. Life History Strategies

*   **Definition:** The traits that affect an organism's schedule of reproduction and survival (e.g., age at first reproduction, number of offspring, lifespan).
*   **r-Selected Species (Opportunists):**
    *   Adapted for rapid growth (high r).
    *   Unstable environments, high disturbance.
    *   Many, small offspring; little parental care.
    *   *Examples: Weeds, insects, bacteria.*
*   **K-Selected Species (Competitors):**
    *   Adapted for living near carrying capacity (K).
    *   Stable environments, low disturbance.
    *   Few, large offspring; high parental care.
    *   *Examples: Elephants, whales, humans, oak trees.*
