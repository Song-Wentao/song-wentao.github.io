### 2. Behavior as an Adaptation

*   **Premise:** Behavior is a phenotype. It has a genetic basis and is subject to natural selection.
*   **Behavioral Ecology:** The study of the ecological and evolutionary basis for animal behavior.
*   **The Question:** How does a behavior increase an individual's **inclusive fitness** (its genetic contribution to future generations)?

---

### 3. Foraging Behavior: Finding Food Efficiently

*   **Optimal Foraging Theory:** Natural selection should favor foraging behaviors that maximize energy gain *minus* energy cost and risk.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Animals are not always maximizing calories; they are optimizing a trade-off.
    *   *Example: A shorebird eating clams.*
        *   **Benefit:** Energy from the clam.
        *   **Costs:** Energy to find, open, and digest it; risk of predation while handling it.
    *   The theory predicts which clam sizes are most profitable to eat.

---

### 4. Altruism and Kin Selection

*   **Altruism:** A behavior that reduces an individual's fitness while increasing the fitness of another.
*   **The Puzzle:** How can a self-sacrificing trait evolve?
*   **Hamilton's Rule & Kin Selection:** Altruism can evolve if the beneficiary is a **relative**.
    *   **Hamilton's Rule:** `r * B > C`
        *   `r` = coefficient of relatedness (e.g., 0.5 for full sibling, 0.125 for cousin)
        *   `B` = benefit to the recipient
        *   `C` = cost to the altruist
    *   By helping a relative reproduce, an individual helps pass on shared genes *indirectly*.
*   *Example: Worker honey bees are sterile but help their queen mother reproduce, passing on their own genes indirectly.*

---

### 5. Mating Systems and Sexual Selection

*   Mating systems are influenced by the distribution of resources and mates.
*   **Types:** Monogamy, polygyny (one male, many females), polyandry (one female, many males), promiscuity.
*   **Sexual Selection:** A form of selection that arises from differences in mating success (rather than survival).
    *   **Intrasexual Selection:** Competition *within* a sex (e.g., male-male combat).
    *   **Intersexual Selection:** Mate choice *between* sexes (e.g., females choosing males with the brightest tails). This can lead to the evolution of extreme traits (e.g., peacock's tail).
