### 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, you will be able to:
*   Define and distinguish between an organism’s habitat and its ecological niche.
*   Explain the difference between a fundamental niche and a realized niche.
*   Describe key types of adaptations that allow organisms to survive in their environment.
*   Understand the concept of phenotypic plasticity.

---

### 2. Habitat: The "Address"

*   **Definition:** The place where an organism lives; its environment characterized by physical and biological features.
*   **It's a description of location.**
*   *Example: The habitat of a bluebird is an open field with scattered trees for nesting.*

---

### 3. Niche: The "Profession"

*   **Definition:** The role a species plays in its community; its total use of biotic and abiotic resources.
    *   **Eltonian Niche (1927):** The species' role in the community (e.g., predator, decomposer).
    *   **Hutchinsonian Niche (1957):** A multi-dimensional hypervolume defining the range of conditions under which a species can persist.
*   **It's a description of function.**
*   *Example: The niche of a woodpecker includes eating insects from tree bark, creating nest cavities, and dispersing seeds.*

---

### 4. Fundamental vs. Realized Niche

This is a critical concept for understanding species interactions and distributions.

*   **Fundamental Niche:** The full range of environmental conditions and resources a species *could* use in the absence of competitors, predators, or pathogens.
*   **Realized Niche:** The actual range of conditions and resources a species *does* use due to interactions with other species (e.g., competition, predation).

*   **Classic Example: Connell's Barnacles**
    *   *Chthamalus* barnacles can survive high on rocks (fundamental niche).
    *   But they are outcompeted by *Balanus* barnacles in the lower zones.
    *   Their **realized niche** is only the upper intertidal zone where *Balanus* can't survive desiccation.

---

### 5. Adaptation: Matching the Niche

*   **Definition:** A heritable trait that enhances an organism's survival and reproduction in a specific environment. Result of natural selection.
*   **Types of Adaptations:**
    *   **Morphological:** Physical structure (e.g., camouflage, beak shape).
    *   **Physiological:** Internal functions (e.g., venom production, antifreeze proteins in fish).
    *   **Behavioral:** Actions (e.g., migration, hibernation, mating dances).

---

### 6. Phenotypic Plasticity

*   **Definition:** The ability of a single genotype to produce different phenotypes (observable characteristics) in response to environmental conditions.
*   **Example: The Water Flea (*Daphnia*)**
    *   In a environment without predators, it has a typical rounded head.
    *   In a environment with chemical cues from predators, it grows defensive "helmets" and spines, making it harder to eat.

---

### 7. Key Terminology

*   Habitat
*   Ecological Niche
*   Fundamental Niche
*   Realized Niche
*   Adaptation (Morphological, Physiological, Behavioral)
*   Phenotypic Plasticity
