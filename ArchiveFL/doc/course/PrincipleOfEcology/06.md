### 2. The Interaction Web

Species do not exist in isolation. Their distributions and abundances are shaped by interactions with other species.

| Interaction | Effect on Species 1 | Effect on Species 2 |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Competition** | - | - |

### 3. Competition (-/-)

*   **Definition:** Interaction where species compete for a resource that is in limited supply.
*   **Intraspecific:** Competition *within* a species. Major driver of density-dependent regulation.
*   **Interspecific:** Competition *between* different species.

#### The Competitive Exclusion Principle

*   **Gause's Principle:** Two species competing for the exact same limiting resources cannot coexist indefinitely. One will be a better competitor and eventually eliminate the other.

#### Coexistence: Niche Differentiation

*   **Resource Partitioning:** Natural selection drives competing species to use resources differently. This reduces niche overlap and allows for coexistence.
*   *Classic Example: Anole lizards in the Caribbean. Different species perch on different parts of trees (ground, trunk, high canopy), reducing competition for space and insects.*

---

### 4. Predation (+/-)

*   **Definition:** Interaction where one species (the predator) kills and eats another (the prey).

#### Predator Adaptations

*   **Morphological:** Claws, teeth, fangs, venom, speed, camouflage.
*   **Behavioral:** Hunting strategies (ambush, pursuit), pack hunting.

#### Prey Adaptations

*   **Morphological:** Armor, spines, thorns, camouflage (cryptsis), warning coloration (aposematism).
*   **Behavioral:** Fleeing, alarm calls, living in groups, mimicry.

---

### 5. Predator-Prey Cycles

*   **Lotka-Volterra Model:** A simple mathematical model that predicts oscillating cycles of predator and prey populations.
    1.  Prey population increases.
    2.  Predator population increases (more food).
    3.  Prey population decreases (heavy predation).
    4.  Predator population decreases (starvation).
    5.  Cycle repeats.
*   *Famous Example: Lynx and Snowshoe Hare cycles in the Canadian boreal forest (though the cycle is also influenced by food availability for the hare).*
