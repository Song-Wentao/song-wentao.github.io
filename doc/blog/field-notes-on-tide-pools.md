Low tide hit at 7:40am, so I got there a little before sunrise. Cold rocks, calm water, and more going on in the shallow pools than I expected.

![A shallow tide pool at dawn](img/blog/tide-pool-1.jpg)

The first pool had a colony of anemones tucked into a crack, closed up tight against the air until the water came back. A few hermit crabs were working their way along the bottom, and one very unbothered sea star had wedged itself into a corner.

![Sea star wedged in a rock crevice](img/blog/tide-pool-2.jpg)

What struck me most was how much these pools change hour to hour. Temperature swings, oxygen drops, salinity shifts as the sun evaporates the shallow water — the animals here are dealing with more environmental stress in one tide cycle than most marine species see in a season.

## Notes

- Best visibility was in the first hour after the tide turned
- Bring a wide lens; most of what's interesting is inches away, not far off
- Tide charts for this stretch of coast run about 15 minutes behind the ones for the harbor a mile north
