## Why Study Rocks?

Rocks are the **foundation of every landscape**.

*   They determine the soil type.
*   The soil determines the plants.
*   The plants determine the animals.
*   To understand the biology of a place, you must first understand its geology.

---

## The Rock Cycle

*(Insert a clear, simple diagram of the rock cycle here)*

*   **Igneous:** Formed from cooled magma/lava.
*   **Sedimentary:** Formed from compressed sediments.
*   **Metamorphic:** Changed by heat and pressure.
*   It's a continuous, billion-year process.

---

## Igneous Rocks: Born of Fire

**Formation:** Cooling and solidification of molten rock.

*   **Intrusive (Plutonic):** Cooled slowly *inside* the Earth.
    *   **Example: Granite** - Large, visible crystals (e.g., quartz, feldspar).
*   **Extrusive (Volcanic):** Cooled quickly *on the surface*.
    *   **Example: Basalt** - Fine-grained, often dark. **Obsidian** - Volcanic glass.

---

## Sedimentary Rocks: Storybooks of Earth

**Formation:** Pieces of other rocks (sediments) are eroded, transported, deposited, and compacted over time.

*   They often contain **fossils**!
*   **Example: Sandstone** - Made of sand grains.
*   **Example: Shale** - Made of compacted mud.
*   **Example: Limestone** - Often made from marine shells.

---

## Metamorphic Rocks: Changed by Earth's Forces

**Formation:** Existing rocks are changed by intense heat and pressure.

*   **Example: Slate** (from Shale) - Splits into flat sheets. Perfect for old roof tiles!
*   **Example: Marble** (from Limestone) - Recrystallized, often beautiful and swirlly.
*   **Example: Schist / Gneiss** - Can have beautiful banding and sparkly minerals (mica).

---

## How to Identify Minerals

Minerals are the building blocks of rocks. Identify them using simple tests:

1.  **Color:** Least reliable (many minerals have variations).
2.  **Luster:** How it reflects light (metallic vs. glassy vs. dull).
3.  **Streak:** The color of its powder (scratch it on unglazed porcelain).
4.  **Hardness:** Can it scratch glass? Can a fingernail scratch it? (Mohs Scale).
5.  **Cleavage:** How it breaks (along flat planes vs. irregularly).

---

## Common Minerals to Know

*(Show high-quality photos of each mineral)*

*   **Quartz:** Hard (scratches glass), often clear/white/pink, no cleavage.
*   **Feldspar:** Also hard, often pink/white, has good cleavage.
*   **Calcite:** softer, fizzes with weak acid (vinegar!).
*   **Mica:** Very soft, peels into thin, flexible sheets.
*   **Hematite:** Reddish-brown streak, often metallic.

## Observing Geology in the Field

You don't need to be a geologist to read the landscape.

*   **Outcrops:** Look for road cuts, river banks, cliffs.
*   **Boulders & Bedrock:** Are they the same? Is the bedrock visible?
*   **Glacial Erratics:** Large boulders moved by glaciers, different from local rock.
*   **Stratification:** Look for layers in cliffs—this is sedimentary history!
