# Lecture 09: The Evidence for Evolution
**Textbook Chapter:** Evolutionary Evidence
**Key Concepts:** Fossil Record, Biogeography, Comparative Anatomy, Molecular Evidence

---

## 1. The Consilience of Evidence
*   Evolution is not proven by one single piece of evidence, but by the convergence of evidence from independent fields of science.

---

## 2. The Fossil Record
*   Provides a **direct historical document** of past life.
*   **Shows:**
    *   **Change over time:** Succession of forms (e.g., horse evolution, whale evolution from land mammals).
    *   **Transitional Forms ("Missing Links"):** Fossils with a mix of ancestral and derived traits.
        *   *Example:* *Tiktaalik* (fish -> tetrapod), *Archaeopteryx* (dinosaur -> bird).
*   **Limitations:** Incomplete, biased towards hard-bodied, abundant species in certain environments.

---

## 3. Biogeography
*   The geographic distribution of species.
*   **Evidence:** Species are often closely related to other species in the same region, rather than to species that look similar but live far away.
    *   *Example:* Australian marsupials (kangaroos, Tasmanian devils) are more closely related to each other than to placental wolves or moles elsewhere. This reflects isolation after the breakup of Pangaea.

---

## 4. Comparative Anatomy
*   **Homology:** Similarity in structure due to common ancestry, even if function differs.
    *   *Example:* The pentadactyl limb in mammals (human hand, bat wing, whale flipper, cat leg). Same bones, different functions.
*   **Vestigial Structures:** Remnants of features that served a function in an ancestor.
    *   *Example:* Pelvic bones in whales and snakes, hind limb bones in python skeletons, human tailbone (coccyx), goosebumps.

---

## 5. Molecular Evidence
*   The most powerful and pervasive line of evidence.
*   **Universal Genetic Code:** All life uses the same DNA -> RNA -> protein system.
*   **Conserved Genes:** Critical genes (like ribosomal RNA, Hox genes) are shared across all domains of life.
*   **Molecular Homology:** The same genes with the same functions in different organisms (e.g., *Pax6* eye gene).
*   **Pseudogenes and "Junk DNA":** Shared mistakes are powerful evidence of common descent (e.g., ERVs, GULO pseudogene).

---

## 6. Direct Observation
*   We can observe evolution happening in real-time.
*   *Examples:*
    *   **Antibiotic resistance** in bacteria.
    *   **Pesticide resistance** in insects.
    *   **Industrial melanism** in peppered moths.
    *   **Darwin's finches:** Beak size changes in response to drought/rain.
    *   **HIV and flu virus** evolution.
