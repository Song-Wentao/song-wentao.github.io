# Lecture 05: From Micro to Macro: The Origin of Species
**Textbook Chapter:** Speciation
**Key Concepts:** Species Concepts, Reproductive Isolation, Mechanisms of Speciation

---

## 1. What is a Species? The Species Problem
*   No single definition works perfectly for all organisms.
*   **Biological Species Concept (Ernst Mayr):** "Groups of actually or potentially interbreeding natural populations which are reproductively isolated from other such groups."
    *   **Strength:** Objective criterion (reproduction).
    *   **Weakness:** Doesn't work for fossils, asexual organisms, or some plants that hybridize.
*   **Other Concepts:** Morphological, Phylogenetic, Ecological.

---

## 2. The Engine of Speciation: Reproductive Isolation
*   Biological barriers that prevent two species from producing viable, fertile offspring.
*   **Pre-zygotic Barriers:** Prevent mating or fertilization.
    *   **Habitat Isolation:** Live in different places.
    *   **Temporal Isolation:** Breed at different times.
    *   **Behavioral Isolation:** Different courtship rituals.
    *   **Mechanical Isolation:** Physical incompatibility.
    *   **Gametic Isolation:** Sperm and egg don't recognize each other.
*   **Post-zygotic Barriers:** Prevent the hybrid zygote from developing or reproducing.
    *   **Reduced Hybrid Viability:** Hybrids don't develop properly or die young.
    *   **Reduced Hybrid Fertility:** Hybrids are sterile (e.g., mule).
    *   **Hybrid Breakdown:** First-generation hybrids are fertile, but their offspring are weak or sterile.

---

## 3. Mechanisms of Speciation
*   **Allopatric Speciation ("Other country"):**
    *   Geographic isolation is the initial barrier.
    *   **Vicariance:** A physical barrier splits a population (e.g., mountain rise, river formation).
    *   **Dispersal:** A small population migrates to a new, isolated area (e.g., islands).
    *   *Example:* Darwin's finches on the Galápagos Islands.
*   **Sympatric Speciation ("Same country"):**
    *   Speciation **without** geographic isolation.
    *   Much rarer and more controversial.
    *   Often driven by **disruptive selection** based on ecology.
    *   *Example:* Apple Maggot Flies. Some populations shifted from native hawthorn fruit to introduced apples. They now mate on their preferred host plant, leading to reproductive isolation.

---

## 4. The Role of Hybrid Zones
*   A region where two related species meet, mate, and produce hybrids.
*   **Outcomes:**
    1.  **Reinforcement:** Hybrids have low fitness, so natural selection strengthens pre-zygotic barriers to prevent wasteful mating. This completes the speciation process.
    2.  **Fusion:** The barriers break down, and the two species fuse back into one.
    3.  **Stability:** Hybrids continue to be produced in a stable zone.

---

## 5. Tempo of Speciation
*   **Gradualism:** Species diverge slowly and steadily over long periods.
*   **Punctuated Equilibrium (Eldredge & Gould):** Species undergo rapid change when they first bud from a parent species, then experience long periods of little change (stasis). Most change happens at speciation events.
