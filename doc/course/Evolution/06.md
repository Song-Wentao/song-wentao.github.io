**Textbook Chapters:** Evolutionary Processes / Dynamics

## 1. Beyond Survival: The Power of Sexual Selection
*   Selection for traits that increase **mating success**, even at the expense of survival.
*   **Two Main Mechanisms:**
    *   **Intrasexual Selection:** Competition *within* a sex (usually males) for access to mates (e.g., male deer fighting with antlers).
    *   **Intersexual Selection (Mate Choice):** One sex (usually females) chooses mates based on specific traits (e.g., peacock's tail).
*   **The Handicap Principle (Zahavi):** A costly, exaggerated trait (like a huge tail) is an "honest signal" of male genetic quality ("I'm so fit I can survive even with this handicap").
*   **Runaway Selection (Fisher):** A feedback loop where a female preference for a trait and the trait itself become genetically correlated and increasingly exaggerated.

---

## 2. Life History Evolution
*   "How" and "when" an organism is designed to make its living.
*   The trade-offs in the allocation of time, energy, and resources to different fundamental aspects of life:
    *   **Number vs. Size of Offspring:** Many small offspring or a few large, well-invested ones?
    *   **Reproduction vs. Survival:** Reproduce early and die young, or delay reproduction to grow and be more successful later?
    *   **Current vs. Future Reproduction:**
*   *Example:* Oak trees produce thousands of small acorns (high investment in number), while humans produce very few, highly dependent offspring (high investment in quality).

---

## 3. Coevolution
*   Reciprocal evolutionary change between interacting species.
*   **Predator-Prey / Parasite-Host Arms Races:**
    *   Prey evolves better defenses (toxins, camouflage) -> Predator evolves better offenses (detoxifying enzymes, keen eyesight) -> and so on.
    *   *Example:* Rough-skinned newts evolve extreme toxicity -> Garter snakes evolve incredible resistance to the toxin.
*   **Mutualistic Coevolution:**
    *   Both species benefit and evolve in response to each other.
    *   *Example:* Flowers and their pollinators. Flower shape evolves to fit a specific pollinator, which evolves more efficient ways to get nectar.

---

## 4. Evolutionary Arms Races
*   A specific type of coevolution where adaptations and counter-adaptations escalate.
*   **Red Queen Hypothesis (Van Valen):** "It takes all the running you can do, to keep in the same place." Species must constantly evolve just to maintain their relative fitness because the species they interact with are also evolving.
*   *Example:* The constant evolution of flu viruses and our immune systems; the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria.

---

## 5. Evolutionary Trade-offs
*   A key theme in all these patterns. You can't maximize everything.
*   Resources or traits that enhance one component of fitness often come at the cost of another.
    *   **Reproduction vs. Longevity**
    *   **Growth vs. Defense**
    *   **Bigger offspring vs. More offspring**
