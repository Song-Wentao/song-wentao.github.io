## Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive exploration of evolutionary biology, the unifying theory of all life sciences. We will examine the patterns and processes of evolution, from the origin of genetic variation within populations to the formation of new species and the grand patterns of life's history. Topics include natural selection, genetic drift, speciation, phylogenetics, molecular evolution, and the extensive evidence that supports evolutionary theory. The goal is to provide a solid conceptual foundation for understanding how evolution works and why it is central to modern biology.

## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
*   Define evolution and explain the mechanisms that cause it (natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, mutation).
*   Apply the Hardy-Weinberg principle to determine if a population is evolving.
*   Differentiate between species concepts and describe the mechanisms of speciation.
*   Interpret phylogenetic trees and understand how they are constructed.
*   Explain how molecular data (DNA, proteins) provides evidence for evolution.
*   Describe the role of evolutionary theory in fields like medicine, conservation, and agriculture.
*   Critically evaluate scientific evidence and arguments related to evolutionary biology.
