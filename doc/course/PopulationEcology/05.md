## Why Age Matters

*   So far, we've treated all individuals in a population as identical.
*   In reality, **birth and death rates depend heavily on age.**
*   A population's future growth is determined by its **age structure**—the proportion of individuals in different age classes.

---

## What is Age Structure?

*   The distribution of individuals among different age groups in a population.
*   **Pre-reproductive:** Young, not yet breeding.
*   **Reproductive:** Actively breeding.
*   **Post-reproductive:** Old, no longer breeding.
*   Visualized by an **Age Pyramid**.

---

## Types of Age Pyramids

![Age Pyramids](Population_pyramids.png) 

*   **Expanding Pyramid** (broad base): High birth rates, rapid growth (e.g., many developing countries).
*   **Stable Pyramid** (even distribution): Birth rate ≈ Death rate, slow/zero growth.
*   **Contracting Pyramid** (narrow base): Low birth rates, declining population (e.g., Japan, Italy).

---

## Life Tables: A Demographic Tool

*   A table that summarizes the probability of survival and reproduction for each age class in a population.
*   **Key Components:**
    *   **x:** Age interval.
    *   **lₓ:** Proportion surviving to age *x*.
    *   **mₓ:** Average number of female offspring produced by a female at age *x*.

---

## Net Reproductive Rate (R₀)

*   Calculated from a life table: $ R_0 = \sum l_x m_x $
*   **What it means:**
    *   $ R_0 = 1 $: Population is stable. Each female replaces herself.
    *   $ R_0 > 1 $: Population is growing.
    *   $ R_0 < 1 $: Population is declining.

---

## Example: A Theoretical Animal

| Age (x) | lₓ (Survival) | mₓ (Fecundity) | lₓmₓ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | 1.0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 0.5 | 2 | 1.0 |
| 2 | 0.25 | 3 | 0.75 |
| 3 | 0.0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | | | **R₀ = 1.75** |

*   **Interpretation:** On average, each female produces 1.75 female offspring in her lifetime. The population is growing.

---

## Survivorship Curves

*   Graphs that show the pattern of mortality over a lifetime.
*   **Type I:** Low juvenile death, most individuals die old (e.g., humans, elephants).
*   **Type II:** Constant mortality rate across ages (e.g., birds, squirrels).
*   **Type III:** High juvenile mortality, few survive to old age (e.g., oysters, trees, insects).

---

## Application: Conservation & Management

*   **Endangered Species:** A population with no young individuals (an inverted pyramid) is in dire trouble. Conservation efforts must focus on improving juvenile survival.
*   **Pest Control:** The most effective control targets the life stage with the highest mortality (e.g., mosquito larvae).
*   **Sustainable Harvest:** Forestry and fisheries must ensure they don't only harvest reproductive adults, crippling future growth.

---

## Summary

*   **Age structure** determines a population's growth potential.
*   **Life tables** and **survivorship curves** are key tools for understanding demography.
*   Ignoring age structure gives an incomplete picture of population dynamics.
