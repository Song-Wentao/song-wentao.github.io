## From Exponential to Realistic Growth

*   Last lecture: Growth unlimited → Exponential curve (J-shaped).
*   Reality: Growth is limited → **Logistic growth** (S-shaped).
*   This limitation is due to **density-dependent factors**.

---

## What is Density Dependence?

*   A factor whose effect on the population **changes as the population density changes**.
*   **Negative Density Dependence:** Effect increases as density increases (e.g., competition for food). **This regulates population size.**
*   **Positive Density Dependence:** Effect decreases as density increases (e.g., finding mates in a rare species). Also called Allee effect.

---

## Intraspecific Competition

*   **Competition between individuals of the *same* species.**
*   The primary mechanism behind negative density dependence.
*   They compete for limited **resources**: food, water, light, space, mates, nesting sites.

---

## The Logistic Growth Equation

$$ \frac{dN}{dt} = rN \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right) $$

*   $ \frac{dN}{dt} $ = Rate of change in population size
*   $ r $ = Intrinsic rate of increase
*   $ N $ = Current population size
*   $ K $ = **Carrying Capacity** - the maximum population size the environment can sustain indefinitely.

---

## Breaking Down the Logistic Equation

*   $ rN $: The potential for exponential growth.
*   $ \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right) $: The **realized fraction** of that potential.
    *   **If N is small:** $(1 - N/K) \approx 1$ → Growth is nearly exponential.
    *   **If N = K:** $(1 - K/K) = 0$ → Growth stops ($dN/dt = 0$).
    *   **If N > K:** $(1 - N/K) < 0$ → Population declines.

---

## The Sigmoid (S-Shaped) Curve

![Logistic Growth Curve](Logistic_carrying_capacity.png)

*   **Exponential Phase:** Initial rapid growth (N << K).
*   **Transition Phase:** Growth rate slows down as N approaches K.
*   **Plateau:** Population stabilizes at carrying capacity (K).

---

## Carrying Capacity (K)

*   **Not a fixed number!** It can change.
*   **Example:** A drought reduces available water → K decreases. A rainy year → K increases.
*   It represents the environment's ability to support a population.

---

## Example: Yeast in a Culture

*   **Classic experiment by G.F. Gause (1930s).**
*   Yeast cells grow in a sugar solution.
*   Initially: exponential growth as they consume sugar.
*   Later: growth slows as sugar depletes and waste (alcohol) accumulates.
*   Finally: population levels off at K, determined by resource availability and waste toxicity.

---

## The Allee Effect (Positive Density Dependence)

*   **At low densities, population growth *decreases*.**
*   **Causes:**
    *   Difficulty finding mates (e.g., rare, solitary predators).
    *   Lack of cooperative defense (e.g., muskoxen forming a circle against wolves).
    *   Failure to modify the environment favorably (e.g., desert shrubs creating shade for seedlings).

---

## Summary: Density Dependence

*   **Mechanism:** Intraspecific competition for limited resources.
*   **Model:** Logistic growth equation.
*   **Outcome:** Population stabilization around an environmentally determined Carrying Capacity (K).
*   **This is the first step towards understanding population regulation.**
