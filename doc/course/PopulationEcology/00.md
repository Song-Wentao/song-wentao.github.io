## Course Description

This course explores the principles that govern the growth, regulation, and dynamics of biological populations. We will begin with models of single-species population growth and build towards understanding the complex interactions between species, including competition, mutualism, and predator-prey dynamics. Emphasis will be placed on the mathematical foundation of ecological theory, the interpretation of models, and the application of these principles to real-world problems in conservation, wildlife management, and human health.

**Prerequisites:** [e.g., Introductory Biology I & II, or consent of the instructor.]

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1.  Describe and apply fundamental population models, including exponential, logistic, and metapopulation models.
2.  Analyze how life history traits (e.g., age structure, semelparity/iteroparity) influence population growth and dynamics.
3.  Model and predict the outcomes of species interactions, including competition, predation, and mutualism.
4.  Explain the role of density-dependent and density-independent factors in population regulation.
5.  Evaluate how ecological principles apply to current issues such as invasive species, disease outbreaks, and conservation efforts.
6.  Interpret graphical and mathematical representations of ecological data and theory.
