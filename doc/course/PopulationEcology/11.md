## The Classic +/- Interaction

*   **Predation:** An interaction where one species (the **predator**) kills and eats another species (the **prey**).
*   It is a major selective force, driving the evolution of defenses in prey and counter-adaptations in predators.

---

## Adaptations of Predators

*   **Speed & Strength:** Cheetahs, raptors.
*   **Senses:** Sharp eyesight of eagles, hearing of owls.
*   **Stealth & Camouflage:** Praying mantis, leopard.
*   **Venom & Toxins:** Snakes, spiders.
*   **Group Hunting:** Wolves, lions.

---

## Adaptations of Prey (Anti-Predator Defenses)

*   **Cryptic Coloration:** Camouflage to avoid detection (e.g., stick insects).
*   **Aposematic Coloration:** Bright warning colors to signal toxicity (e.g., poison dart frogs).
*   **Mimicry:**
    *   **Batesian:** A harmless species mimics a harmful one (e.g., hoverfly vs. wasp).
    *   **Müllerian:** Two harmful species evolve to look similar (e.g., many stinging insects).
*   **Behavioral Defenses:** Fleeing, alarm calls, living in groups (herds, schools).

---

## Modeling Predator-Prey Dynamics: Lotka-Volterra

*   A pair of equations describing the coupled cycles of predators and prey.
*   **Prey Population:**
    $ \frac{dN}{dt} = rN - aNP $
    *   Grows exponentially - deaths from predation.
*   **Predator Population:**
    $ \frac{dP}{dt} = eaNP - dP $
    *   Grows from converting prey - constant death rate.

---

## Key Parameters of the Model

*   $N$: Prey population size
*   $P$: Predator population size
*   $r$: Prey intrinsic growth rate
*   $a$: Attack rate (searching efficiency) of predator
*   $e$: Efficiency of converting prey into predator offspring
*   $d$: Predator death rate

---

## Predicting Cycles

*   The model predicts **coupled oscillations**:
    1.  Prey ↑ (few predators) → Predator ↑ (lots of food) → Prey ↓ (heavy predation) → Predator ↓ (starvation) → Prey ↑... and so on.
*   The predator population lags behind the prey population.

---

## Real-World Example: Lynx and Snowshoe Hare

*   Revisited from Lecture 4.
*   Data from Hudson's Bay Company fur records shows a ~10-year cycle.
*   The Lotka-Volterra model captures the essence of these cycles.
*   **However,** in reality, the cycle is also influenced by the hare's food supply (plants), making it a more complex, multi-trophic interaction.

---

## Functional Response

*   The relationship between prey density and the number of prey consumed *per predator*.
*   **Type I:** Linear increase (e.g., filter feeders). Rare.
*   **Type II:** Curving rise to a maximum (most common). Due to handling time.
*   **Type III:** Sigmoid (S-shaped). Due to learning or prey switching.

---

## Numerical Response

*   The change in predator population size due to changes in prey density (through reproduction).
*   The Lotka-Volterra model combines the functional and numerical responses.

---

## Stabilizing Factors

*   In the real world, cycles are dampened by:
    *   **Prey Refuges:** Some prey are safe from predators (e.g., hiding places).
    *   **Predator Switching:** Predators switch to alternative prey when one prey species becomes rare.
    *   **Spatial Heterogeneity:** The environment is not uniform, providing patches of safety.

---

## Summary

*   Predator-prey interactions are a key driver of evolutionary arms races.
*   The Lotka-Volterra model provides a foundational understanding of coupled population cycles.
*   Real-world dynamics are more complex, involving functional responses, refuges, and often other species (leading to multi-trophic interactions).
