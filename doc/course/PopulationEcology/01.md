## What is Population Ecology?

*   The scientific study of populations.
*   Focuses on:
    *   **Population size:** How many individuals are there?
    *   **Density:** How many individuals per unit area?
    *   **Distribution:** How are they spaced out (clumped, uniform, random)?
    *   **Demographics:** Birth rates, death rates, age structure.
    *   **Growth and regulation:** What causes populations to increase, decrease, or stay stable?

---

## Why Study Population Ecology?

*   **Conservation Biology:** Managing endangered species (e.g., California condor, black rhino).
*   **Pest Management:** Controlling outbreak species (e.g., locust swarms, agricultural pests).
*   **Human Population:** Understanding and projecting human demographic changes.
*   **Disease Ecology:** Modeling the spread of infectious diseases (e.g., COVID-19).
*   **Fisheries & Wildlife Management:** Setting sustainable harvest limits.

---

## Key Characteristics of a Population

*   **Size (N):** Total number of individuals.
*   **Density:** Number of individuals per unit area or volume.
    *   *Example:* 200 oak trees per square kilometer.
*   **Dispersion:** Spatial arrangement of individuals.
    *   **Clumped:** Most common (e.g., fish in schools, trees around a water source).
    *   **Uniform:** Often due to competition (e.g., nesting birds on a cliff).
    *   **Random:** Rare in nature (e.g., wind-dispersed seeds in a field).

## Introduction to Key Terms

*   **Population:** A group of individuals of the same species living in an area.
*   **Community:** An assemblage of different populations living in an area.
*   **Ecosystem:** The community plus the abiotic factors in the environment.
*   **Biotic Potential:** The maximum reproductive capacity of a population under ideal conditions.
