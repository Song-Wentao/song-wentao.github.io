### **Course Description**

This course explores the patterns of species diversity and the processes that structure ecological communities. We will investigate the theories and empirical evidence behind species interactions (e.g., competition, predation, mutualism), community dynamics (e.g., succession, disturbance), and spatial ecology (e.g., island biogeography, metacommunities). We will synthesize these concepts to understand large-scale patterns of biodiversity, community stability, and the pressing impacts of global change. The course combines lecture, discussion of primary literature, and critical analysis of ecological concepts.

**Prerequisites:** Introductory Biology (BIOL 1101/1102) and Introductory Ecology (BIOL 3300) or permission of the instructor.

---

### **Learning Objectives**

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
*   Describe the key historical and modern theories of community assembly and structure.
*   Explain the mechanisms behind major species interactions and their role in shaping communities.
*   Analyze and interpret patterns of biodiversity, succession, and spatial ecology.
*   Critically evaluate scientific evidence from classic and modern studies in community ecology.
*   Synthesize course concepts to predict how communities will respond to anthropogenic environmental change.
*   Effectively communicate ecological concepts in written and oral formats.

### **Assignment Descriptions**

*   **Problem Sets:** These assignments will give you hands-on practice with the quantitative and conceptual tools of community ecology. You are encouraged to discuss approaches with classmates, but all written work must be your own.
*   **Final Project:** You will choose a topic in applied community ecology (e.g., conservation, restoration, climate change impacts) and write a 5-7 page paper proposing a research study or reviewing the current state of knowledge. A 10-minute presentation to the class is also required.
*   **Exams:** Exams will include a mix of multiple-choice, short answer, diagram interpretation, and essay questions designed to test your understanding and application of course material.
