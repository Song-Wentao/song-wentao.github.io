# **Lecture 06: Spatial Patterns**

**Course:** Community Ecology
**Instructor:** [-]

### **Part 1: Review & The Dimension of Space (10 min)**

*   **From Last Time:** Communities change over *time* (succession).
*   **Today's Focus:** How are communities structured across *space*? No community is an island... but some actually are!
*   **Key Concept:** Dispersal limitation and geography are fundamental to understanding diversity patterns.

---

### **Part 2: The Theory of Island Biogeography (25 min)**

*   **Developed by:** Robert MacArthur & E.O. Wilson (1967).
*   **The Model:** Explains species richness on islands as a dynamic equilibrium between **immigration** and **extinction**.
*   **Two Key Predictors:**
    1.  **Island Size:** Larger islands have lower extinction rates (larger populations, more habitats).
    2.  **Distance from Mainland:** Islands closer to the source have higher immigration rates.

![bg right:40% w:400](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Equilibrium_theory.svg/330px-Equilibrium_theory.svg.png)

*   **The Graph:** The equilibrium point is where the immigration and extinction curves cross.
*   **"Islands" are not just oceanic:** Mountain tops, lakes, forest fragments in a farmland "sea" are all ecological islands.

---

### **Part 3: Metacommunity Ecology (25 min)**

*   **A Metacommunity:** A set of local communities linked by the dispersal of multiple, potentially interacting species.
*   **It combines:** Local processes (competition, predation) with regional processes (dispersal, migration).
*   **Four Perspectives (Frameworks):**
    1.  **Patch Dynamics:** Identical patches; coexistence is maintained by trade-offs between competitive ability and dispersal (e.g., a superior competitor is a poor disperser).
    2.  **Species Sorting:** Patches differ in environment; species have niche specializations and disperse to find their ideal patch.
    3.  **Mass Effects:** High dispersal "rescues" species from local extinction or swamps local adaptation.
    4.  **Neutral Theory:** All species are ecologically equivalent; patterns are driven purely by dispersal, speciation, and stochasticity.

---

### **Part 4: Applying the Theory: Habitat Fragmentation (20 min)**

*   Human activity (logging, agriculture, urbanization) turns continuous habitats into archipelagos of fragments.
*   **This creates "islands" and has two main effects:**
    1.  **Reduces Fragment Size:** Leads to higher extinction rates (small populations are more vulnerable).
    2.  **Increases Isolation:** Reduces immigration rates, preventing re-colonization after local extinction.
*   **Example: The Amazon Rainforest**
    *   The **Biological Dynamics of Forest Fragments Project** (BDFFP) showed that:
        *   Small fragments lose diversity rapidly, especially large predators and understory birds.
        *   Edge effects (changes in microclimate) penetrate deep into fragments.
    *   **Conservation Implication:** Protecting large, well-connected areas is paramount.

---

### **Part 5: Classic Example: The Mangrove Islands (20 min)**

*   **Ecologists:** Daniel Simberloff and E.O. Wilson (1969).
*   **The Experiment:** They defaunated (fumigated) small mangrove islands in the Florida Keys to remove all arthropods.
*   **The Goal:** To test the Island Biogeography model *experimentally*.
*   **The Results:**
    *   Re-colonization was rapid.
    *   Islands closer to the mainland reached higher species richness faster (higher immigration).
    *   Species richness returned to a pre-defaunation equilibrium, but the exact species composition was different—demonstrating the role of chance in assembly.
*   **The Takeaway:** Provided strong experimental support for the dynamic equilibrium model of Island Biogeography.
