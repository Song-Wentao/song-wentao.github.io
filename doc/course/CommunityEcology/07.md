**Course:** Community Ecology

### **Part 1: Review & The Ultimate Pattern (10 min)**

*   **From Last Time:** We understand the local and regional processes that influence diversity.
*   **Today's Focus:** The most dramatic pattern in all of ecology: the **Latitudinal Diversity Gradient (LDG)**.
*   **The Pattern:** Species richness for most taxa increases from the poles to the tropics. It is one of the oldest, most consistent observations in biology.

---

### **Part 2: The Latitudinal Diversity Gradient (LDG) (20 min)**

*   **A Staggering Fact:** A single hectare of tropical rainforest can contain over 600 tree species—more than all of North America!
*   **The pattern holds for:** Trees, birds, mammals, insects, marine invertebrates, parasites.
*   **It is likely the result of many factors working together:** No single hypothesis fully explains it.

---

### **Part 3: Hypotheses Explaining the LDG (40 min)**

#### **1. Historical & Evolutionary Hypotheses**
*   **Time and Area Hypothesis:** The tropics are older and have had more time for speciation. They also cover a larger area.
*   **The Rate of Evolution Hypothesis (Cradle vs. Museum):**
    *   **Cradle:** Higher speciation rates in the tropics (e.g., due to faster metabolic rates or stronger biotic interactions).
    *   **Museum:** Lower extinction rates in the tropics (e.g., stable climate over evolutionary time provides refuge).

#### **2. Ecological Hypotheses**
*   **Productivity-Energy Hypothesis:** More solar energy input supports more individuals, allowing more species to maintain viable populations ("more individuals hypothesis").
*   **Climate Stability Hypothesis:** Stable tropical climates allow for specialization on narrow niches, packing more species into a community.
*   **Spatial Heterogeneity Hypothesis:** The tropics are more complex structurally (e.g., more canopy layers), providing more niches.
*   **Biotic Interactions Hypothesis:** Stronger competition, predation, and mutualism in the tropics drive character displacement and higher rates of coevolution and speciation.

---

### **Part 4: Why Does Biodiversity Matter? Ecosystem Function (15 min)**

*   **The Question:** Does the number of species in a community affect how it functions (e.g., productivity, nutrient cycling, stability)?
*   **The Debate:**
    *   **Rivet Hypothesis:** (Ehrlich & Ehrlich) Each species is like a rivet in an airplane; losing a few might be okay, but losing too many causes collapse.
    *   **Redundancy Hypothesis:** (Walker) Species are redundant; ecosystem function is maintained until the last species in a functional group is lost.
*   **Synthesis from Experiments (e.g., Cedar Creek, ECOTRON):**
    *   **Sampling Effect:** More diverse plots have a higher chance of containing a highly productive species.
    *   **Complementarity:** Different species use resources in complementary ways (niche partitioning), leading to more efficient resource use and higher overall ecosystem function.
    *   **Overall finding:** Biodiversity often has a positive, saturating relationship with ecosystem function.

---

### **Part 5: Classic Example: The Cedar Creek Biodiversity Experiment (15 min)**

*   **Ecologist:** David Tilman and colleagues (1990s-present).
*   **The Experiment:** Planted plots of Minnesota grassland with different numbers of plant species (1, 2, 4, 8, 16...).
*   **The Findings:**
    1.  More diverse plots were **more productive** (higher biomass).
    2.  More diverse plots were **more stable** from year to year (less affected by drought).
    3.  More diverse plots used resources more completely and were better at **invasion resistance**.
*   **The Mechanism:** Primarily **complementarity** (e.g., deep-rooted and shallow-rooted species using different water sources).
*   **The Takeaway:** Biodiversity is not just a result of ecosystem processes; it is a **driver** of those processes.

---

### **Part 6: Wrap-up & Look Ahead (10 min)**

*   **Summary:** The Latitudinal Diversity Gradient is the premier large-scale pattern in ecology, likely caused by a synergy of evolutionary history, climate, and energy. Biodiversity itself is not just a product but a cause of healthy, functioning ecosystems.
