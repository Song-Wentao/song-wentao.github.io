### **Part 1: Review & The Role of Interactions (10 min)**

*   **From Last Time:** We can describe community structure by measuring diversity.
*   **Today's Question:** What **processes** create these patterns?
*   **The most fundamental process:** **Competition** – the struggle for limited resources.

---

### **Part 2: The Theory of Competition (25 min)**

#### **1. Intraspecific vs. Interspecific Competition**
*   **Intraspecific:** Competition *within* a species. Drives logistic population growth.
*   **Interspecific:** Competition *between* different species. The focus of community ecology.

#### **2. G.F. Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle (1934)**
*   **"Complete competitors cannot coexist."**
*   If two species have **identical niches**, one will be a better competitor and drive the other to extinction locally.
*   **Coexistence, therefore, requires niche differences.**

#### **3. Lotka-Volterra Competition Model**
*   A mathematical model describing the outcome of competition.
*   Key parameters: **α** & **β** (competition coefficients).
    *   *α:* Effect of Species 2 on Species 1.
    *   *β:* Effect of Species 1 on Species 2.
*   **Four Possible Outcomes:**
    1.  Species 1 wins.
    2.  Species 2 wins.
    3.  **Stable coexistence** (if each species inhibits itself more than it inhibits the other).
    4.  Unstable equilibrium (winner depends on starting populations).

---

### **Part 3: The Niche Concept and Resource Partitioning (25 min)**

#### **The Ecological Niche**
*   **Hutchinson's N-Dimensional Hypervolume:** The sum total of a species' environmental tolerances and resource requirements.
*   **Fundamental Niche:** The full range of conditions where a species *can* live without competition.
*   **Realized Niche:** The actual range of conditions where it *does* live, limited by competition.

#### **How Do Species Coexist? Niche Differentiation!**
*   Species evolve to use **different resources** to avoid competition.
*   **Mechanisms:**
    *   **Spatial:** foraging in different areas (like MacArthur's warblers).
    *   **Temporal:** being active at different times (day vs. night).
    *   **Resource:** consuming different things (seed size, prey type).

---

### **Part 4: Classic Example: Connell's Barnacles (20 min)**

*   **Ecologist:** Joseph Connell (1961).
*   **Species:** Two barnacles on Scottish coast.
    *   *Chthamalus stellatus:* Higher on rock, more tolerant of drying.
    *   *Semibalanus balanoides:* Lower on rock, superior competitor but less tolerant of drying.
*   **The Experiment:**
    *   **Observation:** *Chthamalus* only lives in the upper zone.
    *   **Question:** Is this due to physical tolerance or competition?
    *   **Method:** Connell removed *Semibalanus* from rocks.
*   **The Result:** *Chthamalus* was able to survive and grow in the lower zone once the competitor was removed.
*   **The Takeaway:** A clear demonstration of:
    *   **Fundamental Niche** of *Chthamalus* (whole shore).
    *   **Realized Niche** of *Chthamalus* (upper shore), restricted by competition.

### **Part 5: Wrap-up & Look Ahead (10 min)**

*   **Summary:** Competition is a powerful force structuring communities. The Competitive Exclusion Principle predicts that coexistence is only possible through niche differentiation.
*   **Connell's experiment** is a foundational example of how clever field experiments can reveal the hidden role of species interactions.
