### **Part 2: Describing a Community: The Basics (20 min)**

#### **1. Species Abundance**
*   How many individuals of each species are present?
*   Usually presented as a **Rank-Abundance Curve**.
    *   *X-axis:* Species rank (most abundant = 1, second most = 2, etc.)
    *   *Y-axis:* Abundance (e.g., number of individuals, % cover)
*   **What it shows:** The level of **dominance** and **evenness** in a community.

#### **2. Species Richness**
*   The simplest measure: the **number of species** present.
*   **Crucial Caveat:** Richness depends on the area sampled and sampling effort. We need standardized methods.

---

### **Part 3: Quantifying Diversity: Beyond Richness (30 min)**

Diversity has two components: **Richness** (number of species) and **Evenness** (how equal their abundances are).

#### **The Shannon-Wiener Index (H')**
*   A common index that combines richness and evenness.
*   $H' = -\sum_{i=1}^{S} p_i \ln p_i$
    *   $S$ = total number of species (richness)
    *   $p_i$ = proportion of individuals belonging to species *i*
*   **Interpretation:** Higher H' = higher diversity. A community with 10 perfectly even species has a higher H' than a community with 1 dominant species and 9 rare ones.

#### **Example: Two Insect Communities**
*   **Community A:** 99 individuals of Species 1, 1 of Species 2. (H' is low)
*   **Community B:** 50 individuals of Species 1, 50 of Species 2. (H' is higher)
*   *Both have a richness of 2, but Community B is more diverse.*

---

### **Part 4: Comparing Communities: Beta Diversity (20 min)**

*   **Alpha Diversity (α):** Diversity within a single, local habitat (what we just measured with H').
*   **Beta Diversity (β):** The difference in species composition **between** habitats.
    *   **Turnover:** The change in species as we move across a landscape.
*   **Why it matters:** It tells us about habitat specialization and connectivity.

#### **Example: A Mountain Slope**
*   **Base of Mountain (α):** Oak and hickory trees, certain birds.
*   **Top of Mountain (α):** Spruce and fir trees, different birds.
*   **Beta Diversity (β) between base and top is HIGH.** There is a lot of turnover. If they had the same species, beta diversity would be LOW.

### **Part 5: Wrap-up & Look Ahead (10 min)**

*   **Summary:** We don't just list species; we quantify their abundance and distribution to describe community structure. Diversity indices like Shannon-Wiener combine richness and evenness. Beta diversity measures turnover between sites.
*   **These measures are our tools** to ask *why* patterns exist. For example, why does one forest have higher alpha diversity than another?
