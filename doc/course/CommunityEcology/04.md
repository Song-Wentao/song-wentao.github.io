### **Part 1: Review & A New Type of Interaction (10 min)**

*   **From Last Time:** Competition (-/-) is a major structuring force, leading to niche differentiation.
*   **Today's Focus:** **Exploitative interactions** (Predation, Herbivory, Parasitism), which are +/- interactions.
*   **Key Question:** How do these "top-down" forces shape communities, often in dramatic ways?

---

### **Part 2: Definitions and Concepts (20 min)**

#### **Types of Exploitative Interactions**
*   **Predation:** A predator kills and consumes its prey (+/-).
*   **Herbivory:** An animal consumes part of a plant or algae, but usually doesn't kill it outright (+/-).
*   **Parasitism:** A parasite lives on/in a host, deriving benefit and harming it, but not immediately killing it (+/-).
*   **Parasitoidism:** (A special case) An insect (e.g., wasp) lays eggs in a host; larvae consume and eventually kill the host.

#### **The "Top-Down" vs. "Bottom-Up" Debate**
*   **Bottom-Up Control:** Community structure is driven by resources ( nutrients, light). "The world is green because plants are limited by nutrients, not herbivores."
*   **Top-Down Control:** Community structure is driven by predators controlling herbivore populations. "The world is green because predators keep herbivores in check."
    *   **Trophic Cascade:** The powerful indirect effect predators have on lower trophic levels.

---

### **Part 3: Trophic Cascades: The Power of Predators (30 min)**

A change in the top trophic level that propagates down through the food web.

#### **The Classic Example: Sea Otters, Urchins, and Kelp Forests**
*   **Trophic Levels:** Sea Otter (Predator) -> Sea Urchin (Herbivore) -> Kelp (Primary Producer)
*   **Scenario 1: Otters Present**
    *   Otters eat urchins.
    *   Urchin populations are low.
    *   Kelp forests flourish, creating habitat for fish and invertebrates.
*   **Scenario 2: Otters Removed (e.g., by historic fur trade)**
    *   Urchin populations explode.
    *   Urchins overgraze kelp.
    *   "Deforestation" of the kelp forest; habitat is lost, biodiversity plummets.
*   **The takeaway:** A single predator species can indirectly maintain an entire habitat.

#### **The Modern Example: Wolf Reintroduction in Yellowstone National Park**
*   Wolves (predator) -> Elk (herbivore) -> Aspen/Willow (vegetation)
*   Since wolf reintroduction: Less elk browsing has allowed riparian (streamside) vegetation to recover, which has stabilized riverbanks and changed the physical landscape.

---

### **Part 4: Defense and Counter-Defense: The Arms Race (20 min)**

Exploitative interactions drive evolutionary arms races.

#### **Plant Defenses Against Herbivory**
*   **Physical:** Thorns, spines, trichomes (hairs), tough leaves.
*   **Chemical:** Toxins (e.g., cyanide, tannins), digestibility reducers.
    *   **Example:** Milkweed plants produce cardiac glycosides, which are toxic to most insects. Monarch butterfly caterpillars have evolved to sequester these toxins, making themselves poisonous to birds.

#### **Prey Defenses Against Predation**
*   **Cryptic Coloration:** Camouflage.
*   **Aposematism:** Warning coloration (e.g., poison dart frogs).
*   **Mimicry:**
    *   *Batesian:* A harmless species mimics a harmful one (e.g., hoverfly vs. wasp).
    *   *Müllerian:* Two harmful species evolve to look similar (e.g., many stinging insects have black-and-yellow stripes).
