### **Part 2: Defining the "Community" (20 min)**

#### **What is a Biological Community?**
A group of **interacting species** inhabiting the same place at the same time.
*   Focus: **Interactions** (competition, predation, mutualism).
*   Examples: A pond community, a coral reef, the soil fauna in a forest.

#### **Two Historical Views**
*   **Frederic Clements (1916):** The **Superorganism**
    *   Communities are like an organism; species work together in a predictable, integrated unit.
    *   **Analogy:** A human body (organs depend on each other).
*   **Henry Gleason (1926):** The **Individualistic Concept**
    *   Communities are chance assemblages of species with similar environmental needs.
    *   **Analogy:** A airport terminal (individuals are together by coincidence, not because they depend on each other).

**Modern View:** A synthesis. Communities are influenced by both environmental filtering (Gleason) and biotic interactions (Clements).

### **Part 3: Key Themes in Community Ecology (30 min)**

1.  **Structure:** Who is there and how many? (Measuring diversity).
2.  **Interactions:** The "web of life" – who eats whom, who competes with whom?
3.  **Dynamics:** How do communities change over time? (Succession after a fire).
4.  **Spatial Patterns:** How are communities connected across a landscape?
5.  **Biodiversity & Function:** Does more species richness lead to a more productive/stable ecosystem?
6.  **Global Change:** How are human activities rewriting the rules of community assembly?

### **Part 4: A Classic Example: The Warblers of Maine (15 min)**

*   **Ecologist:** Robert MacArthur (1958).
*   **The Puzzle:** Five species of warblers coexisted in the same spruce forests of Maine. How did they avoid competitive exclusion?
*   **The Method:** Meticulous observation of foraging behavior.
*   **The Finding: Resource Partitioning.**
    *   Each species foraged in a distinct **niche zone** within the spruce trees.
    *   Some fed on outer branches, some on inner branches, some high in the canopy, some low.

![bg right:40% w:350](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/MacWarblers.svg/330px-MacWarblers.svg.png)

*   **The Takeaway:** Species can coexist by **subdividing resources**, a fundamental mechanism of community structure.
