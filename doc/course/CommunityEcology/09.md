# **Lecture 09: Climate Change and Communities**

**Course:** Community Ecology
**Instructor:** [-]
**Date:** [-]

---

### **Part 1: Review & The Ultimate Disturbance (10 min)**

*   **From Last Time:** Communities can be stable or resilient, but they can also undergo regime shifts past a tipping point.
*   **Today's Focus:** Human-caused climate change is a pervasive, global-scale disturbance. How are communities responding, and what can ecology predict?

---

### **Part 2: Observed Responses to Climate Change (25 min)**

#### **1. Range Shifts**
*   **The General Pattern:** Species are moving **poleward in latitude** and **upward in elevation** to track their preferred climate envelope.
*   **Example:** Edith's Checkerspot Butterfly shifted its range northward and upward in elevation.
*   **Problem:** The climate is changing faster than many species can disperse. Species in flat areas have nowhere to go.

#### **2. Phenological Shifts (Changes in Timing)**
*   **The General Pattern:** Spring events are happening earlier (e.g., budburst, migration, egg-laying).
*   **Example:** Bird migration and caterpillar emergence are becoming **decoupled**. If birds arrive after the caterpillar peak, their chicks may starve. This is a **phenological mismatch**.

#### **3. Evolutionary Adaptation**
*   Some species with short generation times are evolving rapidly.
*   **Example:** Mosquitoes evolving drier climate tolerance, some plants flowering earlier.

---

### **Part 3: Novel Communities and No-Analog Climates (25 min)**

*   **No-Analog Climate:** Future climates may have no modern-day equivalent—combinations of temperature and precipitation that don't exist today.
*   **Novel Communities (Emergent Communities):** As species move individualistically (at their own pace), new combinations of species will assemble that have never interacted before.
    *   What will the new predator-prey, competitive, and mutualistic interactions be? It's a giant ecological experiment.
*   **Example:** The "grizzly-polar bear" hybrid ("grolar bear" or "pizzly bear") is a literal example of species interacting in new ways due to overlapping ranges.

---

### **Part 4: Tipping Points and Amplifying Feedbacks (20 min)**

*   Climate change can trigger regime shifts that then *accelerate* further change.
*   **Example 1: The Boreal Forest Dieback**
    *   Warming -> Drought -> Insect outbreaks -> Forest fires -> Release of stored CO₂ -> More warming.
*   **Example 2: Arctic Albedo Effect**
    *   Warming -> Sea ice melt -> Loss of reflective white surface -> Dark ocean absorbs more heat -> More warming -> More melt.
*   **Example 3: Amazon Dieback**
    *   Warming + deforestation -> Reduced transpiration -> Less rainfall -> Forest becomes savanna -> Release of massive CO₂ -> More warming.

---

### **Part 5: What Can Community Ecology Tell Us? (20 min)**

*   **1. It Helps Us Predict Winners and Losers.**
    *   **Losers:** Specialist species, species with poor dispersal ability, species at the top of mountains or confined to specific ranges.
    *   **Winners:** Generalist species, highly mobile species, invasive species.
*   **2. It Informs Conservation Strategies.**
    *   **Protected Areas:** Need to be designed with climate corridors (connectivity) to allow for range shifts.
    *   **Assisted Migration:** The controversial idea of manually moving species to new, suitable habitats.
    *   **Focus on Processes:** Instead of just protecting specific species, protect the processes that generate diversity and resilience (e.g., predation, disturbance regimes).
