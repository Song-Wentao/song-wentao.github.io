### 2. The Abiotic Template of Life

Life is not distributed randomly on Earth. Its distribution is fundamentally constrained by the **physical environment**—the **abiotic** (non-living) factors.

*   **Key Abiotic Factors:**
    *   Temperature
    *   Water
    *   Sunlight (Light)
    *   Nutrients
    *   Salinity
    *   pH
    *   Wind
    *   Disturbance (e.g., fire, wave action)

---

### 3. Temperature: The Master Factor

*   **Why it matters:** Governs metabolic rates. Most organisms function within a narrow range (0°C - 45°C). Enzymes denature outside tolerable ranges.
*   **Adaptation Example (Polar Bear):**
    *   **Thick fur and blubber:** Superb insulation.
    *   **Black skin:** Under white fur to absorb solar radiation.
    *   **Counter-current heat exchange:** In limbs, arteries warm cold blood returning in veins.

---

### 4. Water: The Solvent of Life

*   **Why it matters:** Essential for all biochemical reactions. The balance between water gain and loss is critical.
*   **Adaptation Example (Kangaroo Rat):**
    *   Lives in deserts, never drinks water.
    *   **Gets water** from metabolizing seeds it eats.
    *   **Conserves water** by producing highly concentrated urine and dry feces.
    *   **Nocturnal behavior** avoids daytime heat.

---

### 5. Law of Tolerance & Limiting Factors

*   **Shelford's Law of Tolerance:** The distribution of a species will be controlled by that environmental factor for which the organism has the narrowest range of tolerance.
    *   *Visual: A bell-shaped curve for performance vs. an environmental gradient.*
*   **Liebig's Law of the Minimum:** An organism's growth is limited by the nutrient that is in shortest supply (the limiting nutrient).
    *   *Example: In aquatic ecosystems, phosphorus is often the limiting nutrient for algal growth.*

---

### 6. From Climate to Biomes

*   **Climate:** The long-term prevailing weather conditions in an area (temperature, precipitation, sunlight, wind).
*   **Biome:** A major life zone characterized by vegetation type (e.g., tropical rainforest, temperate grassland, tundra) or physical environment (e.g., freshwater, marine).
*   **The Connection:** Global patterns of temperature and precipitation are the primary determinants of the major terrestrial biomes.
