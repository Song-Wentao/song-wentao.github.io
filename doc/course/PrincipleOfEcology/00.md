## **4. Course Description**

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the science of ecology, the study of the interactions that determine the distribution and abundance of organisms. We will explore ecological processes at multiple levels of organization, from individuals and populations to communities, ecosystems, and the entire biosphere. Key topics include the physical environment, population growth and regulation, species interactions, community dynamics, energy flow and nutrient cycling, and behavioral ecology. The course will conclude by applying these principles to critical modern issues including conservation biology, landscape ecology, and global change. Emphasis will be placed on the scientific process, critical thinking, and understanding the interconnectedness of ecological systems.

## **5. Learning Objectives**

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

*   Describe the fundamental principles governing ecological systems at individual, population, community, ecosystem, and global levels
*   Explain how abiotic factors constrain the distribution and abundance of life on Earth
*   Model population growth and identify factors that regulate population size
*   Classify species interactions and predict their outcomes for community structure
*   Trace the flow of energy and the cycling of key nutrients (e.g., carbon, nitrogen) through ecosystems
*   Apply ecological theory to real-world problems such as biodiversity loss, habitat fragmentation, and climate change
*   Critically evaluate ecological data, graphs, and scientific literature
