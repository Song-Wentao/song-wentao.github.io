### 2. Communities Change Over Time

Communities are not static. They are dynamic and change over time due to disturbance and internal processes. This process is called **succession**.

*   **Disturbance:** An event that changes a community by removing organisms or altering resource availability (e.g., fire, storm, landslide, human activity).

---

### 3. Primary Succession

*   **Definition:** Succession that begins in a virtually lifeless area where soil has not yet formed.
*   **Starting Point:** Bare rock, sand dunes, volcanic rock, retreating glacier.
*   **Pioneer Species:** The first organisms to colonize. Often lichens and mosses that can break down rock and begin forming soil.
*   **Example:** Succession on glacial moraines in Alaska, documented by ecologist William Cooper.

---

### 4. Secondary Succession

*   **Definition:** Succession that begins in an area where an existing community has been partially or completely removed, but soil remains intact.
*   **Starting Point:** Abandoned farmland, area after a fire or hurricane.
*   **Process:** Much faster than primary succession because seeds, roots, and soil organisms are still present.
*   **Example:** Old-field succession in the eastern US: Weeds → Grasses → Shrubs → Pine Forest → Hardwood Forest.

---

### 5. Mechanisms of Succession

*   **Facilitation:** Early species make the environment more suitable for later species (e.g., fixing nitrogen, providing shade).
*   **Inhibition:** Early species hinder the establishment of later species (e.g., competing for space and light).
*   **Tolerance:** Later species are unaffected by earlier species and simply outcompete them in the changed environment.

---

### 6. The Climax Community: Myth or Reality?

*   **Traditional View (Clemente):** Succession is a predictable, directional process leading to a stable, self-perpetuating **climax community** in equilibrium with the climate.
*   **Modern View (Gleason):** Communities are not superorganisms. Succession is not perfectly predictable. The endpoint is influenced by chance, history, and ongoing disturbances. The concept of a single, stable climax is often replaced with the idea of **non-equilibrium communities** shaped by frequent disturbance.

---

### 7. Intermediate Disturbance Hypothesis

*   **Proposal:** Highest species diversity is maintained at intermediate levels of disturbance.
    *   **Low Disturbance:** Competitive exclusion by dominant species lowers diversity.
    *   **High Disturbance:** Only strong colonizers (r-strategists) can survive.
    *   **Intermediate Disturbance:** Prevents competitive exclusion while allowing time for less competitive (K-strategist) species to establish.
