### 2. What is a Community?

*   **Definition:** An assemblage of populations of various species living close enough for potential interaction.
*   **It's about who is there and how many.**
*   **Key Attributes:**
    *   **Species Diversity:** The variety of different organisms.
    *   **Species Composition:** The identity of the species present.
    *   **Trophic Structure:** The feeding relationships between organisms.

---

### 3. Measuring Biodiversity

Biodiversity is more than just a species list. It has two components:

1.  **Species Richness:** The *number* of different species in a community. The simplest measure.
2.  **Species Evenness (Relative Abundance):** The *proportion* each species represents of the total individuals.

*   **Example:** Two forests each have 100 trees and 10 species (richness = 10).
    *   **Forest A:** 91 maples, 1 of each other species. **(Low evenness, lower diversity)**
    *   **Forest B:** 10 of each species. **(High evenness, higher diversity)**
*   Ecologists combine these into indices like the **Shannon Index**.

---

### 4. Foundation Species

*   **Definition:** Species that play a major role in creating and defining a community structure, often by physically modifying the environment.
*   **They are the "engineers" of the ecosystem.**
*   *Examples:*
    *   **Beavers:** Build dams that create ponds, transforming a river community into a pond community.
    *   **Corals:** Build reefs that provide habitat for thousands of other species.
    *   **Trees in a forest:** Create the physical structure and microclimates.

---

### 5. Keystone Species

*   **Definition:** A species that has a disproportionately large effect on its community relative to its abundance.
*   **Their impact is not related to their biomass, but their ecological role.**
*   **Classic Example: Paine's Sea Star (Pisaster)**
    *   Experiment: Removing the sea star (a top predator) from a tidal zone.
    *   Result: Mussels (its prey) outcompeted and eliminated most other species, dramatically reducing diversity from 15 species to just 2.
    *   The sea star was the keystone that held the entire arch of the community together.

---

### 6. What Controls Community Structure?

A central question in ecology. The answer is usually a mix of:
*   **Abiotic Factors:** Climate, disturbance, geography.
*   **Species Interactions:** Competition, predation, mutualism (the focus of today).
*   **Historical Events:** Dispersal, evolution, chance (e.g., which species arrived first?).
