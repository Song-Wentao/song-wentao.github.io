### 2. What is Global Change?

*   **Definition:** Planetary-scale changes in the Earth system, often driven by human activities.
*   **The "Big Four" Components:**
    1.  Climate Change
    2.  Atmospheric CO₂ Increase & Ocean Acidification
    3.  Ozone Depletion
    4.  Widespread Nitrogen Deposition

---

### 3. Climate Change: The Greenhouse Effect

*   **Natural Greenhouse Effect:** Certain gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O) in the atmosphere trap heat, keeping Earth warm enough for life.
*   **Enhanced Greenhouse Effect:** Human activities (burning fossil fuels, deforestation) are increasing the concentration of these greenhouse gases, trapping *too much* heat.

#### Evidence is Overwhelming
*   Rising global temperatures.
*   Shrinking glaciers and ice sheets.
*   Rising sea levels (thermal expansion + meltwater).
*   Increasing frequency of extreme weather events.

---

### 4. Ecological Consequences of Climate Change

*   **Range Shifts:** Species are moving poleward in latitude and upward in elevation.
*   **Phenological Shifts:** Timing of life cycle events is changing (e.g., earlier flowering, earlier bird migration). This can create **mismatches** (e.g., caterpillars hatch before migrant birds arrive to feed them).
*   **Coral Bleaching:** Warmer water temperatures cause corals to expel their symbiotic algae, turning white and often dying.
*   **Ocean Acidification:** Increased CO₂ dissolves in oceans, forming carbonic acid. This lowers pH, making it harder for marine organisms to build shells and skeletons (e.g., corals, mollusks, plankton).

---

### 5. Other Global Change Factors

*   **Ozone Depletion:** Caused by CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons). The stratospheric ozone layer protects life from UV radiation. The Montreal Protocol is a success story in global action—the ozone hole is slowly healing.
*   **Acid Rain:** Caused by sulfur and nitrogen oxides from burning fossil fuels. Lowers the pH of soils and water, harming forests and aquatic life.
*   **Nitrogen Deposition:** Human-fixed nitrogen from fertilizers and industry is deposited via rain, altering soil chemistry and favoring fast-growing species, which reduces biodiversity.

---

### 6. The Role of Ecology

Ecology provides the scientific foundation to:
*   **Understand** these complex changes.
*   **Predict** future impacts.
*   **Develop solutions** (e.g., restoration ecology, carbon sequestration, designing resilient protected areas).
