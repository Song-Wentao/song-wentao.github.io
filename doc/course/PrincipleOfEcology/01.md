### 2. What is Ecology?

*   **Official Definition:** The scientific study of the interactions that determine the distribution and abundance of organisms.
    *   **Distribution:** *Where* organisms are found and why.
    *   **Abundance:** *How many* organisms are present and why.
*   **Ecology vs. Environmentalism:** Ecology is a **science**. Environmentalism is a **social and political movement** informed by that science.
*   **Etymology:** From the Greek *oikos* (house) and *logos* (study). The "study of one's house."

---

### 3. The Hierarchical Levels of Ecology

Ecology is studied at different levels of biological organization, each with its own set of questions.

1.  **Organismal:** How an individual's structure, physiology, and behavior meet environmental challenges.
    *   *Example: How does a cactus conserve water in the desert?*
2.  **Population:** A group of individuals of the same species living in an area. Focuses on population size and factors that change it.
    *   *Example: What is controlling the wolf population in Yellowstone?*
3.  **Community:** All the populations of different species living in an area. Focuses on species interactions.
    *   *Example: How does the reintroduction of wolves affect elk, beaver, and tree populations?*
4.  **Ecosystem:** The community of organisms plus the physical factors in the environment (energy flow, nutrient cycling).
    *   *Example: How does fire affect nutrient availability in a forest?*
5.  **Landscape & Biosphere:** Multiple ecosystems connected; the global ecosystem.
    *   *Example: How do forest fragments in a farm landscape interact? How does atmospheric CO2 affect global climate?*

---

### 4. How Do Ecologists Do Science?

*   **Observation:** The first step. Noting a pattern in nature.
    *   *Example: In this pond, algae blooms happen after it rains.*
*   **Hypothesis:** A proposed, testable explanation for an observed phenomenon.
    *   *Example: Fertilizer runoff from lawns is causing the algae blooms.*
*   **Experimentation:**
    *   **Manipulative Experiment:** The researcher changes one variable (e.g., adds fertilizer to some test ponds but not others).
    *   **Natural Experiment:** Using natural variation to test ideas (e.g., comparing ponds in neighborhoods with and without lawns).
*   **Models:** Mathematical or conceptual representations of ecological systems used to make predictions.

---

### 5. Why Study Ecology?

*   **Conservation of Biodiversity:** Understanding how to protect endangered species and manage habitats.
*   **Public Health:** Controlling the spread of diseases (e.g., Lyme disease, malaria) which involves hosts, vectors, and the environment.
*   **Natural Resource Management:** Sustainable management of fisheries, forests, and water resources.
*   **Predicting Global Change:** Understanding and mitigating human impacts like climate change, pollution, and invasive species.
