### 3. Small Population Risks: The Extinction Vortex

Small, isolated populations face a downward spiral toward extinction due to:
1.  **Genetic Drift:** Loss of genetic variation by chance.
2.  **Inbreeding Depression:** Mating between relatives increases the chance of harmful recessive traits being expressed.
3.  **Demographic Stochasticity:** Random fluctuations in birth and death rates (e.g., all offspring one year are male).
*   **Minimum Viable Population (MVP):** The smallest population size that can have a high chance of persisting.

---

### 4. Landscape Ecology

*   **Definition:** The study of the ecological processes that operate across large geographic areas (landscapes).
*   **A landscape is a mosaic** of interacting **patches** (e.g., forests, fields, lakes) embedded in a **matrix** (the dominant cover type).

#### Key Concepts:
*   **Habitat Fragmentation:** The process where a large, continuous habitat is broken into smaller, isolated patches. This increases edge effects and reduces core habitat.
*   **Corridors:** Strips of habitat that connect isolated patches. They can help mitigate fragmentation by allowing animal movement and gene flow.

---

### 5. Conservation in Action: Protected Areas

*   **SLOSS Debate:** Single Large Or Several Small reserves?
    *   **Large:** Better for species with large home ranges, lower edge effects.
    *   **Small:** Can protect more habitat types (beta diversity).
*   **Modern Solution:** A network of reserves connected by corridors is often best.
*   **Case Study: The Florida Panther**
    *   Threatened by inbreeding depression (kinked tails, heart defects).
    *   Genetic rescue: Introduced Texas cougars to increase genetic diversity.
    *   Conservation efforts focus on creating wildlife corridors to connect isolated populations.
