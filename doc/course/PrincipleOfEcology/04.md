### 2. What is a Population?

*   **Definition:** A group of individuals of a single species living in the same general area.
*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Geographic Range/Distribution:** The area over which a population occurs.
    *   **Abundance & Density:** Number of individuals, often per unit area.
    *   **Dispersion:** The spacing of individuals (clumped, uniform, random).

---

### 3. Demography: The Vital Statistics

*   **Demography:** The study of vital statistics that affect population size.
*   **Birth Rate (Natality, b):** Number of offspring produced per unit time.
*   **Death Rate (Mortality, d):** Number of deaths per unit time.
*   **Growth Rate (r):** The per capita rate of increase.
    *   `r = b - d`

---

### 4. Exponential Growth: The Power of Unlimited Potential

*   **Assumption:** Resources are unlimited. There are no constraints on growth.
*   **Pattern:** J-shaped curve. The population grows faster and faster as it gets larger.
*   **The Equation:**
    *   `dN / dt = r * N`
    *   **dN/dt:** The change in population size (N) over a change in time (t) (i.e., the growth rate).
    *   **r:** The intrinsic rate of increase (max. growth rate under ideal conditions).
    *   **N:** The current population size.

---

### 5. A Classic (and Cautionary) Example

*   **Reindeer on St. Matthew Island:**
    *   1944: 29 reindeer introduced. Ample food (lichen), no predators.
    *   Population grew **exponentially** to 6,000 by 1963.
    *   Winter 1963-64: Population **crashed** to ~50 animals due to overgrazing and starvation.
    *   **Lesson:** Exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely. It is always stopped by **limiting factors**.

---

### 6. When Does Exponential Growth Happen?

*   Introduced to a new, suitable environment with abundant resources.
*   Rebounding after a catastrophic population decline.
*   In laboratory settings with controlled, ideal conditions.
*   **In nature, it is rare and always temporary.**
